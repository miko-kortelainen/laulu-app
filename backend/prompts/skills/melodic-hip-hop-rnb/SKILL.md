# Melodic Hip-Hop & R&B Lyric Craft

## 1. Emotional Core & Storytelling
- **Intimate Vulnerability Over Generic Flexing:** Center songs on late-night introspection, relationship friction, trust issues, survivor's guilt, emotional numbness, or the isolation behind material success.
- **No Studio-Booth or Hustle-Bro Filler:** Never write generic "rapper in the booth" lines (*"i'm in the booth, the beat is knocking, i spit the truth"*) or corporate grindset slogans (*"build the vision, stack the plans, count the profit"*).
- **Material Details as Emotional Contrast:** When cars, cash, designer clothes, or luxury spaces appear, use them to sharpen human tension (an empty passenger seat, missed texts from someone who knew you before the money, a stocked fridge that still feels lonely)—never as a sterile checklist of safes, coupes, and bank checks.
- **Scene Continuity & Verse Progression:**
  - Keep Verse 1 grounded in one continuous physical scene and emotional moment.
  - Use Verse 2 to shift the angle, advance the timeline, or deepen the relational fallout—never repeat Verse 1's lines in shuffled order.

## 2. Vocal Pocket, Cadence & Phrasing
- **Sung-Rapped Melodic Flow:** Write for a vocalist riding the space between speaking and singing, stretching vowels across the snare rather than marching stiffly on every beat.
- **Vary Line Lengths & Cadence Pivots:** Use 7–12 syllable bars with natural contractions (`ain't`, `don't`, `won't`, `tryna`, `gonna`). Pivot the rhythmic feel every 4 bars—for example, tighter conversational phrasing in bars 1–4 opening into longer, sustained melodic lines in bars 5–8.
- **No Three-Comma Clause Lists:** Never chop bars into three 4-syllable mini-clauses separated by commas (*"we hit the town, the coupe is clean, the tires glide"*). Let single thoughts and enjambed sentences flow naturally across the bar.
- **Phrasing Punctuation (`.` and `,`):** Mark breath and cadence boundaries clearly at the end of bars—use `,` at the end of a bar when a thought floats into the next line after a short breath, and `.` when a melodic statement resolves.

## 3. Rhyme Architecture & Melodic Prosody
- **Open-Vowel & Sonorant Line Endings:** End melodic lines, pre-choruses, and choruses on open vowels or sustained sonorants (`-ound`, `-igh`, `-ay`, `-o`, `-in`, `-all`, `-ere`, `-ame`, `-own`) that a vocalist can hold and pitch-bend. Avoid locking 4 bars into clipped plosive stops like `-ock` (*block/clock/stop/drop*) or `-alk` (*talk/balk*).
- **Multisyllabic & Assonance Rhymes Over Monosyllabic `AAAA`:**
  - Do not write 4-bar blocks of elementary 1-syllable perfect rhymes (*high/lie/fly/try*, *road/load/code/mode*).
  - Build **2–3 syllable vowel chains (assonance and compound rhymes)** across bar endings and internal stresses (e.g. *empty shelves / built ourselves*, *city spins around / rising off the ground*, *passenger side / asking why*).
  - Every line across all 8 bars of a verse must lock into an audible vowel pocket (`AABB CCDD` or `ABAB CDCD`), pairing internal vowel echoes with end-bar rhymes.
