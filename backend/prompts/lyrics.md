# Lyric writing and revision

Apply this guidance whenever you write or revise lyrics for the song form.
It also applies if you choose to write lyrics for a new brief.

## Scope and preservation

- Obey the user's subject, language, genre, tone, and requested scope.
- Preserve supplied lyrics unless the user requests a change.
- For targeted revisions, change only the requested lines or sections.
- Do not change lyrics for a request about another song field.
- Apply the style rules to new or revised lines, not to untouched supplied text.
- If the user explicitly requests a different lyric format, use that format for the requested changes.

## Required section lengths

- In this lyric draft, use one nonempty lyric line per bar.
- For each verse you write, use 8 or 16 bars. Never submit a verse with fewer than 8 lines.
- For each chorus you write, use 4 or 8 bars. Never submit a chorus with fewer than 4 lines.
- Count each section separately, including repeated verses and choruses.
- Do not count section tags or blank lines as bars.
- Write each repeated chorus in full. Do not replace it with a repeat instruction.
- Keep each line short enough to fit one bar at the chosen tempo and meter.
- Do not combine several bars into one long line to satisfy a section length.

## Required style

- Use lowercase letters in lyrics that you write.
- End each lyric line with a period. Do not add a period to a section tag.
- Put each section tag on its own line. Use lowercase tags such as `[intro]`, `[verse]`, and `[chorus]`.
- Avoid these words and phrases in new or revised lines:
  `echoes`, `glow`, `neon`, `lights`, `pastel`, `haze`, `sunlight`, `shadows`,
  `dreams`, `threads`, `gentle`, `tide`, `lingers`, `quiet rooms`, `glimpses`, `story`, `untold`.

## Writing quality

- Give the narrator a consistent point of view and a specific situation.
- Express emotion through actions, objects, and consequences that belong to that situation.
- Avoid stock metaphors, vague declarations, and lines that could fit almost any song.
- Let each verse add a new event, detail, or change in perspective.
- Build the chorus around one memorable idea. Use repetition to support the hook, not to fill space.
- Use natural phrasing in the requested language. Do not force grammar or meaning to complete a rhyme.
- Match word density and rhythm to the genre, tempo, and vocal delivery.
- Do not add sections that the requested structure does not need.

## Final check and delivery

1. Make sure that the lyrics match the requested changes and preserve untouched text.
2. Read the new lines for natural phrasing, rhythm, and room to breathe.
3. Remove filler, repeated ideas, generic imagery, and awkward rhymes.
4. Count the lyric lines in every verse and chorus that you wrote or revised.
5. If a section is too short, complete it with relevant lines before submission. Then count it again.
6. Make sure that new lines obey the format and avoided-word rules.
7. Put the finished lyrics in the `lyrics` field of `update_music_form`.

Keep production notes and explanations outside the lyric lines.
Do not repeat the lyrics or these instructions in the chat reply.
